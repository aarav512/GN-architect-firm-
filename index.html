The studio

Architecture begins where emotion meets function.

We began in 2014 as Guru Nanak Architects. The name is shorter now. The work is the same: rooms that stay calm when the day is not, plans that can be built, and materials chosen for how they age in Delhi light.

A house is a sequence. Arrival, shade, a court, a table, a window that knows the afternoon. We draw that sequence until it feels inevitable, then we stay close while it is made.

Delhi NCR · Since 2014

Practice

Five drawings,
one practice.

Open a sheet. Each panel unfolds the way a drawing does — line first, then the room.

Houses and apartments planned around courts, cross-ventilation, and the way a family actually lives. From the first sketch to the last sample on site.

Furniture, light, and joinery drawn with the architecture, not after it. Kitchens, bedrooms, and rooms for gathering, edited until nothing is merely decorative.

Studios, galleries, and small workplaces that borrow the calm of a house. Clear plans, durable materials, and a public face that does not shout.

Older Delhi houses, opened carefully. We keep what still works — a verandah, a good wall — and redraw the rest for light and present life.

Before a line is committed: site reading, brief, feasibility, and a clear sequence of decisions. Useful whether or not we go on to build.

Process

A measured path from the first conversation to the site.

  1. 01

    Consultation

    We visit, listen, and write the brief in plain language: how you live, what the plot allows, what must endure.

  2. 02

    Concept Sketch

    A few plans, not twenty. The idea is tested in plan, section, and a handful of material notes.

  3. 03

    3D Visualization

    Models used to decide, not to impress. Light, proportion, and the feel of a room before it is priced.

  4. 04

    Technical Planning

    Drawings a contractor can build from. Structure, services, joinery, and a schedule that respects the city.

  5. 05

    Execution Support

    Site reviews, sample approvals, and the small corrections that keep the built work faithful to the drawing.
